## Fit and eligibility checks before you bid

Read each opportunity's requirements before you commit time. These checks come up again and again in financial services tenders:

1. **FCA authorisation.** Insurance broking, banking, payment services, credit and investment advice are regulated activities. Expect to show you hold the right FCA permissions for the work, not just for your firm in general.
2. **Audit registration.** Where an audit is statutory, the buyer will expect a firm registered to carry out statutory audit work, and named responsible individuals.
3. **Professional indemnity insurance.** Buyers usually set a minimum level of PI cover. Check it against your current policy early, because raising cover takes time.
4. **Frameworks.** Some opportunities are run as frameworks rather than single contracts. Getting onto a framework can open the door to later call-off work, so treat it as a long-term investment.
5. **Independence and conflicts.** Audit and advisory work often asks you to confirm you have no conflicts with the buyer or its other advisers.
6. **Experience and references.** Expect to show similar work for comparable organisations, ideally in the public sector.

### What financial services do public buyers buy?

Mostly audit and accountancy, insurance and insurance broking, banking and payment services, and financial advice. At the time of writing, audit and accountancy was the largest group, with 9 of the 21 open opportunities.

### Do I need FCA authorisation to bid?

For regulated work such as insurance broking, banking, payment services or investment advice, yes: buyers will expect the right FCA permissions. Audit and accountancy work instead depends on professional registration, especially for statutory audit.

### How much professional indemnity insurance do I need?

It depends on the contract. Each buyer sets its own minimum, so check the requirement in the tender documents before you bid.

### Are financial services tenders suitable for small firms?

Often, yes. At the time of writing, 5 of the 21 open opportunities sat in the £100k–£250k band, which suits a smaller practice or specialist broker.
