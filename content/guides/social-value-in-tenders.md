## Why everyone is talking about social value

Social value has become one of the most argued-about parts of UK public procurement this year. One side says weighting social value pushes contracts towards local jobs and skills. The other says it adds a hidden cost to every bid, favours firms with large bid teams and crowds out price and quality. Government has responded by narrowing the model rather than dropping it. The new model focuses on two outcomes, good jobs and skills, and cuts the number of delivery outcomes from eight to two ([Pinsent Masons on PPN 026](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/uk-government-procurement-bolster-local-jobs-skills)).

For a small firm the practical point is simple. Social value questions aren't going away, the rules are changing, and a clear, honest answer can win marks that larger competitors lose with generic copy.

## What social value means in a tender

In a tender, social value is the wider benefit your business will create while delivering the contract, over and above the work itself. Buyers include a question about it in the quality section and score your answer against published criteria.

There are two sets of rules to know.

- **Central government.** Departments, their executive agencies and non-departmental public bodies follow a procurement policy note. Since February 2025 that has been [PPN 002](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ppn-002-taking-account-of-social-value-in-the-award-of-contracts), which set a minimum 10% weighting ([Muckle LLP](https://www.muckle-llp.com/insights/legal-commentary/ppn-social-value/)). For procurements starting on or after 1 January 2027 it is replaced by PPN 026.
- **Councils and other public bodies.** PPN 026 doesn't apply to them, although they may choose to adopt it. In England, councils buying services must consider, before they start, how the contract could improve the economic, social and environmental well-being of their area ([Public Services (Social Value) Act 2012, section 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2012/3/section/1)). They must also have regard to the [National Procurement Policy Statement](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-procurement-policy-statement). Expect a social value question in many council tenders, with weightings set by each council.

## The social value weightings for 2027

PPN 026 applies to covered procurements under the Procurement Act 2023 with a total contract value of £1m or more, including VAT. A procurement counts as started when its tender notice is published ([PPN 026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ppn-026-the-social-value-model/ppn-026-the-social-value-model-html)).

| Contract value (inc. VAT) | Minimum social value weighting |
|---|---|
| Above the public contract threshold but under £1m | 0% |
| £1m to under £5m | 10% |
| £5m or more | 20% |

Source: [Guidance on using PPN 026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ppn-026-the-social-value-model/guidance-on-using-ppn-026-the-social-value-model-html). Where the buyer uses a price per quality point or value for money method, the 10% or 20% applies to the quality score rather than the total.

For contracts of £5m or more, the buyer should set at least one social value key performance indicator (KPI), and report on performance at least once every 12 months. Poor performance against social value KPIs can be taken into account when deciding whether to exclude a supplier from future tenders.

## What the new social value question asks

PPN 026 replaces a wide menu of themes with two outcomes and six award criteria.

**Outcome 1: good jobs**
- Create or retain high-quality jobs
- Fair working conditions
- Fair pay above the statutory minimum

**Outcome 2: skills**
- Training and retraining
- In-work progression
- A talent pipeline, such as apprenticeships, work placements and pre-work training

The model question asks you, within a word limit, to set out the specific, measurable and time-bound commitments you'll make to deliver the chosen outcome in the relevant area. You can answer in one of two ways ([Guidance on using PPN 026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ppn-026-the-social-value-model/guidance-on-using-ppn-026-the-social-value-model-html)):

1. **Support a listed community programme.** Show it with a dated letter to the programme's contact, confirming you'll support it if you win and that the support is additional to anything you already do.
2. **Design your own commitment.** Explain how it meets the criteria, who the target cohort is, how you'll reach them and tailor the offer, and how you'll involve staff, suppliers and communities.

Either way, you also provide a method statement covering:

- the commitment, with quantities, milestones and deliverables you'll be measured against
- how you'll prepare and support the people taking part
- how you'll end the activity in a way that helps them progress, for example signposting further training or jobs
- how you'll publish your commitments and performance

## How social value answers are scored

This is the part many bidders get wrong. Under PPN 026 the buyer scores how well your method statement and implementation plan will achieve the outcome, not the size of the number you promise. The quantity you commit to still goes into the contract and is monitored, but promising more people doesn't by itself earn more marks. The guidance says the model is designed to avoid creating barriers for new entrants, SMEs and voluntary sector organisations.

So a small firm offering a modest, well-planned commitment can outscore a large firm offering big numbers with a vague plan. Councils running their own social value questions may score differently, so always read the scoring descriptors in the tender documents.

## How to write a social value answer that scores

1. **Read the question and the criteria twice.** Note the outcome the buyer chose, the target cohort, the area, the word limit and the weighting.
2. **Link it to the contract.** Award criteria must relate to the subject matter of the contract, so tie your offer to the work, the sites and the people involved in delivering it.
3. **Pick one or two commitments you can really deliver.** It's better to commit to something small that you'll definitely do than something large you can't staff.
4. **Make it specific, measurable and time-bound.** State what, how many, for whom, where and by when.
5. **Explain the how.** Cover recruitment or referral routes, who in your team owns it, how you'll support participants and how the activity ends.
6. **Show how you'll report.** Name the measure, how often you'll report and to whom. Use the buyer's standard metrics where the documents give them.
7. **Add evidence.** A short example of similar activity you've already delivered makes the plan believable.
8. **Price it in.** Social value commitments become contract terms. Make sure your price covers them.

### A social value answer structure you can adapt

- **Commitment:** We will [offer X work placements / recruit X apprentices / pay all contract staff at least X] for [target cohort] in [area] during [period].
- **Why it fits:** This supports [outcome and award criterion] because [link to the contract and local need].
- **Delivery:** [Named role] will lead. We will reach participants through [referral partner or route] and support them with [induction, mentor, adjustments].
- **Ending well:** At the end of each placement we will [signpost further training, offer interviews, provide references].
- **Reporting:** We will report [metric] to the contract manager every [quarter] and publish a yearly summary.

Fill in the brackets with real figures you can deliver. Don't copy the same answer into every bid. Evaluators can tell.

## Social value tips for small firms

- **Your size can be an advantage.** Plans that are local, personal and easy to check read well against the new criteria.
- **Use what you already do.** Fair pay, flexible working, training and trade union access can all count under the good jobs criteria if they go beyond the statutory minimum and apply to the contract workforce.
- **Start relationships early.** The guidance encourages buyers to point suppliers towards community programmes during market engagement, so make contact before the tender lands.
- **Check the weighting before you bid.** A tender where social value is a fifth of the marks needs a proper answer. Factor that into your [bid or no bid decision](/guides/bid-no-bid-decision).

## FAQ

### What is social value in a tender?

It's the wider benefit a supplier commits to deliver through a public contract, such as good jobs, fair pay, training and apprenticeships. Buyers score it as part of the quality assessment.

### What is the social value weighting in UK tenders?

From 1 January 2027, central government contracts under PPN 026 need a minimum 10% weighting from £1m to under £5m, and 20% at £5m or more. Councils set their own weightings.

### Does PPN 026 apply to council tenders?

No. It applies to central government departments, their executive agencies and non-departmental public bodies. Councils may adopt the same approach, and in England they must consider social value when procuring services under the Public Services (Social Value) Act 2012.

### Do bigger social value commitments score more marks?

Not under PPN 026. Buyers score the quality of your method statement and plan, not the quantity you commit to. The quantity becomes a contract commitment that's monitored.

### Can a small business compete on social value?

Yes. The model is designed not to create barriers for SMEs. A focused, realistic plan tied to the contract often scores better than a long list of generic promises.
